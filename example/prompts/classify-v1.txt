INTENTS

- question:        they want to know how something works.
- bug_report:      something is broken for them and they are telling us.
- billing:         charges, invoices, plan changes, refunds.
- feature_request: they want something the product does not do.
- complaint:       dissatisfaction that is not a specific bug.
- acknowledgement: a closing courtesy ("thanks", "got it"). Needs no reply.
- spam:            not a customer message.

PRIORITY RULES. These exist because each one was a real misroute, and they override the
plain reading of the intent list above.

1. A QUESTION INSIDE A COMPLAINT IS STILL A QUESTION. If they are unhappy AND they asked
   something specific, route to the question: answering it is what reduces the unhappiness.
2. "IT DOESN'T WORK" WITHOUT SPECIFICS IS A COMPLAINT, NOT A BUG REPORT. A bug report names
   what they did and what happened. Without that, the reply has to ask, and those are
   different replies.
3. A REFUND REQUEST IS BILLING even when it arrives wrapped in a complaint. The money
   question is the one that has to be answered.
4. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT IS ONLY FOR A PURE CLOSING COURTESY. "Thanks, but one more thing" is not
   an acknowledgement. Getting this wrong closes a live conversation.
5. CONFIDENCE IS ABOUT THE MESSAGE, NOT ABOUT YOU. Use "low" when two intents genuinely fit
   the same text, not when you personally find it hard. Low confidence is a routing signal
   for a human, so inflating it makes the signal useless.

REASONING is read by a human reviewer deciding whether to trust the route. Write what
actually decided it, not a restatement of the intent name.
